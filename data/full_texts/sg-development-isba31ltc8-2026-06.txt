Note by the secretariat
I. Introduction
1. This report provides the Legal and Technical Commission with an overview of the
development of Standards and guidelines in the context of Council deliberations, with a view to
assisting the Commission in addressing the requests made by the Council during the first part of
the thirty-first session.
II. Background on the development of Standards and guidelines
2. The Commission may recall that the development of Standards and guidelines has featured
in discussions on the draft exploitation regulations since around 2014. During those early
discussions, several stakeholders considered that the technical and administrative aspects of the
regulatory framework for exploitation, would be more appropriately addressed through the
relevant Standards and guidelines, given the flexibility that approach would afford with respect
to amending or updating them in response to improved scientific knowledge or technological
developments1. To that end, the Commission may recall that the report of the President of the
Council for the twenty-third session noted that priority should be given to the adoption of
the draft regulations, while Standards and guidelines could be further developed and/or
updated after 2020.2
3. Substantive work on standards and guidelines commenced with the note on the content
and development of standards and guidelines for activities in the Area under the Authority’s
regulatory framework, prepared by the secretariat for consideration by the Council during the first
part of its twenty-fifth session in 2019. 3 The note was informed by comments received on the

critical Standards and guidelines to be developed in parallel with the regulations and, importantly,

4. In 2019, the Council requested the Commission to examine critical issues with respect to
the provisional and non-exhaustive list of Standards and guidelines contained in ISBA/25/C/3 and
make subsequent recommendations to the Council. To support the Commission in this task, a
workshop was convened by the Secretariat in Pretoria, South Africa, from 13 to 15 May 2019.
5. On the basis of the report prepared following the workshop, the Commission made the
following recommendations to the Council:
(a) The terms “Standards” and “guidelines” should be understood in the context of draft
regulations 94 and 95 of the draft regulations on exploitation of mineral resources in the Area (at
the time ISBA/25/C/WP.1) i.e., Standards were regarded as mandatory, whereas guidelines were
recommendatory.
(b) An outcome-based approach should be used in the development of standards and
guidelines, noting that an outcome-based approach provides for rigorous and contractually
binding outcomes, whilst affording flexibility in the processes used to achieve those
outcomes;
(c) Standards and guidelines should be developed in phases, as follows:
(i) Phase 1: Completion by the time of the adoption of the draft regulations;
(ii) Phase 2: Completion prior to the receipt of the first application for a plan of
work for exploitation;
(iii) Phase 3: Completion by the time of commencement of commercial mining
activities;
(d) Six sets of guidelines should be developed and work on three additional sets should
be initiated in phase 1;
(e) Two technical working groups, led by members of the Commission and including an
appropriate number of recognized experts in the field selected on the basis of article 165,
paragraph 2 (e), of the Convention, should be established in 2019 to support the development of
several environmental guidelines;
(f) Members of the Commission and the secretariat would prepare draft environmental
goals, objectives and principles to support the development of standards and guidelines.
6. The Commission also recommended processes for the development of standards and
guidelines. These included a step to allow for stakeholder consultations and comments. The
adoption of Standards by the Council and their approval by the Assembly had been considered in
the suggested process.
7. During the Twenty-Sixth session, the President of the Council noted in his statement on
the work of the Council5 that, whilst delegations recognized the importance of the Commission's
work on the development of Standards and Guidelines, some expressed a preference for these
instruments to be developed as part of an integrated regulatory package together with the draft
exploitation regulations. A roadmap for advancing work on Standards and Guidelines was
elaborated in that context, following which a number of phase 1 Standards and Guidelines were
subsequently developed. In the decision of the Council on the report of the Chair of the
Commission for that year, the Council expressed its intention to make every effort to ensure the

9. At the twenty-seventh session, the Commission delivered to the Council for its
consideration ten draft Standards and Guidelines under phase 1, based on several rounds of
stakeholder consultation. 7 During deliberations, delegations again shared divergent views: some
suggested that the review of draft Standards and Guidelines should proceed together with the
corresponding provisions of the draft exploitation regulations,8 while others preferred to revisit
the draft Standards and guidelines at a later stage, when the draft regulations were more stable.9
In its decision ISBA/27/C/44, the Council emphasized that the regulations, standards and
guidelines must be an integrated package in order for them to be developed into requirements for
submitted plans of work for exploitation, and that the phase 1 draft standards and guidelines
required in-depth consideration by the Council, as well as review, to ensure consistency with the
draft regulations.
10. At the twenty-ninth session, discussions in the informal working group on the protection
and preservation of the marine environment, and following requests from several delegations to
revisit the issue of Standards and Guidelines, the Secretariat prepared a matrix to link the
environmental standards and guidelines to the relevant provisions of the consolidated text of the
draft exploitation regulations. This work was further developed and formed the basis for the
updated draft list of Standards and Guidelines subsequently presented to the Council at the
thirtieth session.
11. Since the twenty-seventh session, no further work has been carried out by the Commission
on the Standards and guidelines, pending a more definitive structure of the draft regulations on
exploitation. At the thirtieth session, the Council conducted a high-level discussion on Standards
and guidelines based on the updated draft list of Standards and/or guidelines associated with the
draft regulations on exploitation of Mineral resources in the Area10, without however adopting a
decision on it.
12. At the first part of its thirty-first session, the Commission considered the status of work
relating to the development of standards and guidelines and agreed to map the current proposed
list of standards and guidelines, identifying necessary updates to existing drafts and addressing
any potential duplication and would facilitate the structured and timely completion of the relevant
Standards and guidelines. It was considered that this exercise would enable the Commission to
be prepared to commence substantive work once guidance had been received from the Council
on the next steps to be taken and their scope whilst at the same time noting that resources would
be required to support the processes.11
13. In light of the foregoing, the Council during the same thirty-first session, endorsed the
proposal of the President to request the Commission to: 12
(a) to take up the current list of Standards and Guidelines;
(b) to prepare an updated and consolidated version, including merging specific Standards
and/or Guidelines, where appropriate;
(c) to revise the list of Standards and Guidelines that should be ready by the time of the
adoption of the Regulations;

III. Recommendations
14. The Commission is invited to take note of the present report and pursuant to the request
of the Council:
(a) Based on ISBA/30/C/CRP.5, prepare an updated and consolidated version, including
merging specific Standards and/or guidelines, where appropriate;
(c) revise the list of Standards and guidelines that should be ready by the time of the
adoption of the Regulations;
(d) identify those Standards and guidelines that are already prepared and that may
require revision in light of the current state of negotiations; and
(e) develop a timeline or roadmap and recommendations on the development of
Standards and guidelines by the Commission to be reported to the Council.
